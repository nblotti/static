(Instruction for the LLM: generate all dialogue in **English**. Alex is the journalist/moderator. Alex must call the experts **by name** when handing over or asking follow-ups. Alex should let an expert finish, briefly rephrase, then ask the other expert — or the same expert if needed.)

---

### 1. Context setting + reference for the paper

**Alex**

Podcast-style intro. For example:

* “Hello and welcome to **Arxiv LLM Daily EN**.”
* “This is the show where we look at a new arXiv paper every day and try to keep up with the ridiculous speed of AI innovation — but in a way that stays practical.”
* “Today we’re going to dig into a paper and see what it actually changes for people building or using AI.”

Then:

1. Give the reference:

   * Title of the paper
   * (optionally) date / version
   * e.g. “The paper we’re looking at today is…”
2. Explain why we’re talking about it now: “this paper proposes…”
3. Present the two angles, **by name**:

   * “Marc will look at it from the field, real-world usage.”
   * “Jamie will look at the AI / technical side.”
4. Value question:

   > “Concretely, what’s the use of what they’re proposing, and what need does it meet today?”

Then hand over **by name**:

* “Marc, let’s start with what need you’re actually seeing on the ground.”
* After Marc speaks, Alex rephrases and calls Jamie: “Jamie, from the AI side, why is this kind of approach showing up now?”

**Marc**

* Describe the real need it could address.

**Jamie**

* Reframe from the AI side (why this approach is appearing now).

---

### 2. What is the paper actually proposing?

**Jamie**

* Describe the contribution: what it is, where it plugs in, what it’s for.
* Position it compared to what was done before.

**Alex**

* Rephrase in everyday English.
* Then call **Marc** by name.

**Marc**

* Say what it would change in actual usage.

---

### 3. Use cases (practical projection of the paper)

**Alex**

* Transition: “If we applied it in a real team, Marc, what would it look like?”

**Use case 1 – simple**

* **Marc** gives a very operational case.

(After Marc, Alex rephrases and sets up the second case.)

**Use case 2 – more advanced**

* **Marc** describes, **Jamie** connects it to the mechanics of the paper.

**Alex**

* Keeps the thread and hands speaking time back around **by name**.

---

### 4. Under the hood / limitations (according to the paper)

**Jamie**

* Explain prerequisites, assumptions, technical constraints, limitations mentioned by the authors.
* Possible future work.

**Alex**

* Translate that into simple terms.
* Then call **Marc** by name.

**Marc**

* Add the setup / org part.

---

### 5. Results mentioned in the arXiv paper

**Jamie**

* Recap the reported results:

  * benchmarks / datasets,
  * metrics (EM, F1, faithfulness, cost, latency…),
  * comparison to baselines.
* Specify if the setting is very controlled.

**Alex**

* Translate these numbers into understandable benefits.
* Then call **Marc** by name.

**Marc**

* Say what we can expect in a real environment (often a bit worse).

---

### 6. Conclusion

**Marc**

* Closing remark from the field: who benefits the most / under what conditions.

**Alex**

* Final conclusion: what the paper really brings in one sentence

The output must be the complete dialogue in **English**, respecting the order, the roles, and the names.
